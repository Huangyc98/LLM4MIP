How Much Can LLMs Help Solve MIPs?

A research partner for finding structure - not a replacement for solvers or proof

Across 112 open MIPLIB instances, an LLM-assisted campaign produced verified primal progress, stronger historical lower bounds, and 30 supported global conclusions. The useful question is not whether a model can sound clever, but where its ideas change the mathematical state space and survive independent checking.

The question is not whether a language model can emit a plausible optimization idea. It is whether that idea can survive contact with a real mixed-integer program.

The short answer#

LLMs helped materially as research copilots. They were most useful for recovering semantics from large formulations, proposing alternative state spaces, designing bounded experiments, writing extractors and checkers, and coordinating the path from a conjecture to a certificate. They did not replace branch-and-bound, SAT solving, exact enumeration, or independent verification.

30 conclusionsThe reconciled archive supports 28 optimal values and two infeasibility results among 112 studied instances.
25 primal advancesRelative to MIPLIB v36: 21 improved incumbents plus four first finite incumbents.
41 dual advantagesRelative to displayed values in a frozen historical COPT 10-hour table, not a same-hardware race.

These are overlapping outputs of one nonrandom research campaign. They are not 96 distinct wins, not an estimate of what an LLM would solve on a random MIP, and not proof that the language model alone caused each result. Instance choice and research effort were deliberately uneven.

The honest claim: LLM assistance expanded the search for useful representations and proof routes. Solvers and deterministic checkers decided what counted.

Where the LLM helps#

The campaign worked because three kinds of machinery played different roles.

Language model

Reads formulation patterns, connects them to algorithms or theorems, proposes reductions, generates experiments, looks for counterexamples, and helps build proof workflows.

Optimization backends

Search for feasible solutions, numerical bounds, reduced-model optima, and small counterexamples. Their output is evidence only within the scope actually solved.

Independent checkers

Audit the untouched MPS, replay exact dynamic programs or proof traces, test mapping directions, and reject attractive claims that do not survive verification.

This division matters. A model can suggest a parity argument or a SAT encoding; it cannot make the argument true by confidence. Conversely, a generic solver can deliver a strong numerical bound without revealing the structural idea that makes a portable proof possible.

One repeatable research loop#

The recurring pattern is to make the question smaller before spending more computation:

understand the original model → propose a falsifiable structural idea → test it on a bounded pilot → build separate primal and dual artifacts → return to the original MPS → assign the evidence grade actually earned

Four MIPs, four forms of help#

Representation changeA network design MIP becomes a tree dynamic program#

dfn-bwin-DBE is a ten-node fixed-charge multicommodity network design problem. Positive demand for every ordered pair implies that every feasible installed support is connected. Ten or more edges already cost more than the incumbent, so any optimum must be a nine-edge spanning tree.

The LLM-assisted move was to stop treating the formulation as a generic flow MIP and organize the remaining search around tree cuts. Removing an edge determines the cross-cut demand and therefore the cheapest admissible capacity. Two exact subset dynamic programs then covered every spanning tree, not merely the trees reached by a heuristic.

Verified result: the exact lower bound and an original-MPS witness meet at 73,623.79. The solver backend executes; the decisive contribution is the new state space and its auditable mapping.

Semantic recoveryA scheduling formulation reveals an interval order#

fhnw-binschedule2 contains 497 items, six machines, 114 ordered positions, and thousands of auxiliary variables. Reading the selector windows as intervals exposes a 35-element antichain. Combined with two endpoint machines and parity of their loads, that structure forces a makespan of at least 2,428.

Verified result: the public solution attains 2,428 and passes exact checking of all 6,503 original rows. An earlier, more intuitive energy-cut interpretation was wrong and was withdrawn after counterexamples exposed a missing duration term.

Certificate constructionA packing cutoff becomes a checked SAT refutation#

bppc6-06 has a known placement of height 208. Instead of asking a solver for another long optimization run, the workflow isolated the finite question: can height 207 occur? Exact subset weights forced every positive-width profile segment of such a placement into a tight equality, making a strong Boolean encoding possible.

The resulting CNF has 414,391 variables and 1,541,322 clauses. CaDiCaL reported unsatisfiable, drat-trim checked the proof, and a separate replay checked 1,001,496 LRAT lemmas through the empty clause.

Verified result: the cutoff refutation plus the height-208 witness establishes the optimum. The full 2.65 GiB proof bundle is external to Git and identified by hashes, so complete third-party replay requires obtaining or regenerating it.

Invariant reconstructionAn 84,376-variable model closes with parity#

allcolor58 has 197,154 constraints, yet its decisive lower bound fits in three sentences. Every legal package has even capacity. Exactly 42 stores have odd total demand. Each such store must incur at least one unit of mismatch cost.

The value 42 and a construction seed were already known externally; the project does not claim first discovery. Its contribution was to reconstruct the invariant, turn the seed into a full witness, and run two exact audits of every variable, domain, and original row.

Verified result: lower bound 42 and upper bound 42. Here the LLM-assisted value lies in explanation, reconstruction, and auditability rather than authorship of the underlying value.

How strong is the evidence?#

A global conclusion enters the inventory only when its lower and upper sides support the claim on the original formulation. The archive deliberately keeps different evidence classes visible.

Verification is not delegated to another language model. Primal claims are checked against the original MPS; dual claims rely on mathematical arguments, exact computations, checked proof traces, or solver bounds with their numerical status stated. A reduced-model optimum, an infeasible neighborhood, an unchecked UNSAT, or a time limit remains a scoped result.

The strongest comparison is mixed#

A separate historical 20-instance study compared a structure-focused workflow with a workflow that did not invoke the same research skill. It is the closest available comparison, and it points in both directions.

11 of 20better selected primal values for the structure arm; the remaining nine tied under the study's rule.
2 vs 0exact closures recorded by the structure arm versus the comparison arm.
12 of 20stronger dual bounds for the comparison arm; it also had the smaller common gap on 11.

Six selected values were tolerance-indexed rather than strict incumbents. Against strict public baselines, the improvement count was four for the structure arm and two for the comparison arm. The runs were not randomized, blinded, or equal-budget: machines, starts, solver states, and stopping decisions differed. This is operational evidence for a hybrid workflow, not a causal effect size.

The result is useful precisely because it is not a one-sided victory. LLM-guided structural work showed an advantage in primal construction and exact closure; generic solver effort often kept the better numerical dual bound. A sensible system protects both routes.

Where LLMs did not help#

The archive contains attractive ideas that failed: parsers missed indented RHS records, pseudo-Boolean rows were oriented incorrectly, relaxations were mistaken for liftable equivalences, local infeasibility was overread as global, and incomplete proof logs stopped before a final certificate. The corrected FHNW argument is especially instructive: an LLM-readable semantic story was still mathematically wrong until an original-model counterexample broke it.

Eighty-two studied instances remain globally unresolved. Many have useful bounds, verified reductions, local exclusions, or proof kernels, but none of those is a closure. Repeated local repair, longer time limits, and confident explanations do not turn restricted evidence into a global theorem.

A failure becomes scientifically useful when it narrows the next experiment. The workflow records the precise target, transformation direction, time limit, terminal state, and check performed. That ledger prevents the model from quietly recycling a disproved mechanism.

The bottom line#

The project does not show an LLM replacing a MIP solver. It shows something more practical: language models can help researchers read opaque formulations, discover lower-dimensional combinatorial structure, coordinate experiments, and turn promising ideas into proof obligations.

The most valuable AI contribution was often not a final number. It was a smaller question whose answer could be independently checked.

That is how an 84,376-variable distribution model becomes parity, a network design MIP becomes a tree dynamic program, a scheduling formulation becomes an interval order, and a one-unit packing gap becomes a checked SAT refutation. The LLM helps change the representation; solvers and proof systems make the answer reliable.
