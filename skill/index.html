From 112 cases to reusable workflows

Better feasible solutions. Stronger global bounds.

We distilled research records and failure cases from 112 MIPLIB instances into two reusable skills. This is a workflow written as instructions and reference material, not model training. The subsequent evaluation uses 20 different instance names; related problem families may still transfer.

Primal-skill

Find and improve feasible solutions: validate incumbent transfers, organize structural neighborhoods, repair continuous recourse, lift candidates to the original model, and continue searching after an improvement.

Download experimental primal-skill

Dual-skill

Strengthen valid global bounds: derive safe relaxations, valid cuts and decompositions, exhaust complete cutoffs, and independently check the scope of each certificate.

Download experimental dual-skill

What “primal” and “dual” mean in this comparison

Primal vs primal-skill compares the historical no-skill AI's P with the dedicated primal-skill run's P. Dual vs dual-skill compares the same no-skill AI's D with the dedicated dual-skill run's strongest Gurobi / certified D. Lower P and higher D are better. The baseline is an AI research workflow using solvers, not a pure solver or a newly run objective-specific control.

Skill experiments ran in five batches of four different instances with batch barriers. They did not run the primal and dual skills online together on each instance. These are historical comparisons with unequal resources, not randomized or equal-budget causal estimates.

What the results support

Primal-skill: 9 wins, 9 ties, 1 loss

One of the 20 candidates is excluded: ns1456591's reported 988.1403051801293 lies below the independently proved optimum 988.14128344. The original raw table counted it among 10 wins; this page keeps it visible but does not credit it as an improvement.

Dual-skill: 17 wins, 1 tie, 2 losses

Fourteen instances have independent certified bounds, nine improve their certified-bound trajectory, and two close exactly. Strongest numerical bounds and independently certified bounds are separate columns.

Complementary bounds reduce gaps

The two-skill portfolio has a smaller gap on 18/20 cases versus the no-skill AI, averaging 15.8079 percentage points less. Against historical COPT, it wins 19 and loses 1, including three wins by finding a feasible solution where COPT recorded none. Against direct Gurobi 150 minutes, it wins 17, ties 1 and loses 2, including one feasibility win. Mean gap reduction remains 19.2762 percentage points over the 19 finite Gurobi pairs.

Gap = (P − D) / max(1, |P|, |D|). Bound comparisons use absolute tolerance 1e-7; gap comparisons use 1e-9 in fraction units. Mean changes are percentage-point differences, not relative percentages. A solver run without a feasible solution loses to a skill result with a valid feasible solution. Such feasibility wins count in outcomes but not in mean numerical gap reductions. COPT has 17 finite-gap pairs; three cases record no primal, including cdma, which aborted after 0.1 seconds. COPT version, hardware and full model hashes are not available for a matched comparison.

Combination rule: select the lowest valid P and highest valid D after independent runs, using Gurobi and independent certificates only. Exclude the invalid ns1456591 candidate and use the dual run's exact feasible lift. This is a post-hoc portfolio with a larger total search budget, not measured online parallel acceleration.

Primal acceptance retains historical numerical tolerances, mainly 1e-6; additional GMP checks used explicit 1e-5 row/objective and 1e-4 integrality tolerances. Results were not uniformly revalidated at zero tolerance. The two exact closures are already in the 132-instance campaign and must not be counted twice.
