LLM4MIP Skills

The downloadable primal-skill and dual-skill help LLMs analyze instance structure, design solver experiments, and verify the resulting primal and dual progress. Primal-skill improves feasible solutions; dual-skill strengthens global bounds.

Skill focus

How the skill helps LLM-assisted MIP solving

  • Find useful structure: identify exploitable patterns, primal heuristics, and dual certificates instead of relying only on generic solver tuning.
  • Run targeted experiments: turn structural observations into focused tests with an optimization solver.
  • Verify every result: check candidate solutions and bounds against the original model, using independent verification to determine what can be claimed.

Comparison design

What was compared

Primal-skill and dual-skill arms

Dedicated primal-skill and dual-skill runs on the same 20 instances, mainly using Gurobi 13.0.2 with 150 minutes per instance. Primal-skill used 12 CPU slots; dual-skill used 8 solver threads. Their best valid bounds are combined after the runs.

Comparison arm

An archived comparison workflow on the same 20 minimization instances that did not use this specific skill. It still used computational tools and problem-specific methods; it is not a “solver-only” control.

Both skill experiments used five batches of four instances, with checkpoints every 30 minutes and batch barriers. The comparison arm is the historical no-skill AI workflow. Solver comparisons take the best valid bounds from the recorded solver runs. Downloads above are current skills; the frozen primal-skill and frozen dual-skill versions produced these experimental results.

Design limit: this is a paired retrospective reconstruction, not a randomized, blinded, equal-resource A/B experiment. Machines, budgets, solver states, starts, baselines, tolerances, stopping decisions, and research order may differ. The page reports descriptive outcome and workflow differences, not a net causal effect.
