Deep dive 02 · skill-based improvement

Structure first, then solve

The downloadable milp-structure-research skill turns structural investigation into a gated workflow. Across 20 historical paired cases, the skill-guided arm recorded stronger primal outcomes and exact closures—but did not dominate dual-bound progress.

Download the skill Explore the results

The intervention

A research discipline, not a solver preset

Nine tracked skill files—including the profiler—plus the MIT license are packaged in one 21 KiB download.

The skill requires a researcher to freeze the claim and baseline, inspect the untouched matrix, recover semantics, classify the bottleneck, propose at least two falsifiable hypotheses, run bounded pilots, separate construction from proof, map back to the original model, and grade the resulting evidence.

Profile

Read domains, rows, sparsity, scale, symmetry, and possible graph or decomposition structure without optimizing.

Hypothesize

Write competing structural explanations and the smallest experiment that could disprove each one.

Separate

Run primal construction and dual/proof work as distinct pipelines with explicit budgets.

Audit

Lift candidates to the untouched MPS, replay certificates, preserve failures, and state the evidence boundary.

Version used in the experiment: SKILL.md SHA-256 f8a09b104f0af3786b2e702f60dde3de7fd54a1aac581dff9bdd8a3325c84b4f. All 20 skill-arm instances recorded a pre-access skill-read gate.

Experiment design

What was compared

Skill-guided arm

Run controlled_miplib20_20260911_1451: Codex gpt-5.6-sol at ultra reasoning, Gurobi 13.0.1, eight solver threads per instance, and a 32 GiB soft cap. Two disclosed cases used Windows/Gurobi 13.0.2.

Comparison arm

A user-designated historical report on the same 20 minimization instances that did not use this specific skill. It still used computational tools and problem-specific methods; it is not a “solver-only” control.

Models and public baselines were frozen on 11 September 2026. The five batches ran at most four instances in parallel, with batch barriers and no transfer of unused time. Batch 1 allowed 40–120 active minutes per instance; batches 2–5 allowed 90–150. The skill run accumulated 1,729.5301 active minutes and 1,010.2245 overlapping solver minutes.

Design limit: this is a paired historical reconstruction, not a randomized, blinded, equal-resource A/B experiment. Machines, budgets, solver states, starts, baselines, tolerances, stopping decisions, and research order may differ. The page reports association and mechanism—not a net causal effect.

Observed outcomes

The result is useful—and mixed

Switch metrics to see which side of the MIP each workflow advanced.

Interpretation: the skill-guided arm was strongest at finding selected primal points, producing strict public-baseline improvements, and turning two open gaps into exact certificates. The comparison arm more often preserved a stronger numerical dual bound and smaller common gap. That division argues for a hybrid workflow.
